# Scientific writing

Write for the stated reader and purpose. Begin with the question or finding that matters, then explain the evidence and why it supports the conclusion. Give unfamiliar terms a useful definition where they first appear. Keep reasons and connectives when shortening text.

Read the supplied sources before making source-specific claims. Distinguish observations, statistical estimates, model predictions and proposed mechanisms. Preserve numbers, units, bounds, comparison groups and the meaning of the sample size. State a material limitation plainly. Do not invent results, methods or references to complete a narrative.

Use [the style guide](references/style-guide.md) when choosing voice, emphasis or label wording. For English/Japanese or bilingual work, read [the translation guidance](references/english-japanese.md). These references are local and do not require another skill or a particular tool.

Adapt the structure to the task. A research explanation often needs context, question, approach, result, interpretation and unresolved work, but a short caption may need only a result and its essential qualifiers. Cite claims close to the supporting source. Identify missing or conflicting information instead of silently resolving it.

Before delivery, read the text as a colleague outside the specialty. Check that each conclusion has its reason on the page, every quantity retains its context, and each citation supports the attached claim. Report any source or language review that remains incomplete.
