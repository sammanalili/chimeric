# Use a quiet style that keeps the evidence clear

Follow the user's brief, existing document and destination requirements first. The defaults here are adjustable choices, not publication specifications.

## Write direct explanations

Use concrete subjects and active verbs. Explain what was studied, what changed, compared with what, and why that matters. Define technical terms by what they do. State uncertainty with its reason, such as limited observations, an imprecise estimate or an untested mechanism. Keep internal build and review details outside the reader's text.

Use a finding as a heading when the evidence supports it. A topic heading is appropriate when the section introduces background or several unresolved results. Avoid forcing every paragraph into the same template. Let the audience and content determine the amount of explanation.

## Keep visual meaning consistent

Prefer a white background, dark text and generous spacing. A useful starting palette is ink `#1F2933`, gray `#52616B`, blue `#0076B3` and red `#D62724`. Use accent colors sparingly and keep one meaning per color. Preserve the colors and proportions of reproduced evidence panels. Encode essential distinctions with labels, shapes or line patterns as well as color.

Use an available, licensed, readable sans-serif font. For Japanese, use a font with complete Japanese glyph coverage. Do not assume a bundled font exists. Choose text size at the final viewing size, keep a clear hierarchy, and repair crowded layouts before reducing type.

## Keep reader labels honest

Define units, error bars, abbreviations and meaningful arrows or shading. Explain whether a drawing is schematic or to scale. Label illustrative data and uncertain relationships explicitly. Keep text, quantitative marks and annotations editable when the format allows it.

Inspect the actual exported output when producing a file. Check clipping, line breaks, contrast and missing characters. A successful export command does not establish readability or scientific correctness.
