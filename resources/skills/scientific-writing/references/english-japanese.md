# Translate meaning and preserve the scientific claim

Use this reference for English/Japanese translation, bilingual captions, or documents that must stay consistent across both languages.

## Establish the meaning before translating

Use the source paragraph and its scientific context together. Identify the subject, comparison, measured quantity, result and limitation before choosing target wording. Follow an existing terminology list when one is supplied. Keep a small project-local term list only when repeated specialist terms need it; do not turn complete sentences into glossary entries.

Retain names, identifiers, citations, units, signs, inequalities, denominators and uncertainty. A confidence interval, a prediction interval and a range are different quantities. A statistical association must not become a causal claim in translation. Preserve whether an explanation is demonstrated, supported, possible or speculative.

## Write natural Japanese

Use established scientific terms and ordinary Japanese sentence structure. Avoid copying English noun stacks or omitting the subject when that makes the comparison ambiguous. Explain an unfamiliar technical term by its function. Keep one register within a document, following the audience and supplied material rather than imposing a fixed style.

Use consistent half-width numbers and scientific unit symbols where appropriate. Keep names, formulas and identifiers intact. Break lines at natural phrase boundaries. Avoid starting a line with closing punctuation or leaving an opening bracket at a line end. Inspect the rendered output because a line-breaking rule alone cannot judge natural phrasing.

## Choose a useful bilingual layout

Follow the requested lead language and format. A presentation can use one language on the slide and the other in speaker notes. Side-by-side or stacked text is useful when the reader needs both at once, but it should not make the text too small. Keep data marks, axes, units and other non-text meaning consistent across language versions. Reflow text rather than shrinking one language to fit the other's word count.

## Review the target text against the source

Read the target on its own for clarity, then compare it with the source for meaning and every scientific quantity. Use a back-translation for an ambiguous or consequential passage when it helps reveal a changed claim; a back-translation is not proof of accuracy. If source wording has two plausible meanings, retain or explain that uncertainty instead of selecting one silently.

Keep terminology consistent across captions, figures, notes and the main text. Describe the review actually performed. Agent review does not establish native-speaker or specialist human review.
